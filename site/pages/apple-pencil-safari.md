# How to write on a live web page in Safari with Apple Pencil

To write on a web page in Safari on iPad, install the free PageInk app and turn on its Safari extension. Then touch the page with Apple Pencil and write. The ink sits on the live page, not on a screenshot, and it comes back when you reload.

## How do I set it up on my iPad?

You do this once. The app needs iPadOS 17 or later. First install PageInk from the [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6837846057). Then turn on its extension in one of two ways.

**From Safari, on iPadOS 17 or later:**

- Tap the page menu button in the address bar, tap **Manage Extensions**, and turn on **PageInk**.
- Open the page menu again, tap **PageInk**, and choose **Always Allow** and **Always Allow on Every Website**.

**From Settings, on iPadOS 18 or later:**

- Open **Settings**, tap **Apps**, then **Safari**, then **Extensions**.
- Tap **PageInk** and turn on **Allow Extension**.
- Under **Permissions**, tap **All Websites** and choose **Allow**.

Then open any web page in Safari and write on it with Apple Pencil. Safari's wording can differ a little between iPadOS versions.

The app shows these steps under **Set Up Safari**, with a button that opens Settings.

## Do I need to switch anything on for the Pencil?

No. The Pencil is meant to write as soon as it touches the page, while a finger still scrolls the page and taps links. Until the drawing tools have loaded, PageInk holds the Pencil's touches back, so the first stroke after a page loads writes instead of scrolling.

## Why does the page scroll instead of writing?

A common cause is that **All Websites** is still on **Ask**. Safari then does not let PageInk work on a page until you tap its button. The PageInk menu says what to tap, and the app's **Set Up Safari** screen says the step is not done. Choose **Always Allow on Every Website** from the page menu, or set **All Websites** to **Allow** in Settings.

## Can I write with a finger?

Yes. Open PageInk from Safari's address bar. It may sit inside the page menu there. Tap **Draw on this page**, and the button then reads **Drawing on**. One finger now writes and two fingers scroll. Tap the button again to scroll with one finger. The **Draw** button on the bar on the page does the same.

## Where did the PageInk button go?

When you scroll, Safari can shrink its toolbar and hide the button. Tap the thin line with the address at the top to bring the toolbar back. On some iPadOS versions you can also turn off **Hide Tab Bar While Scrolling** in the Safari settings.

## What can I do with the Pencil?

- Write and draw in two pen colours.
- Highlight words. A straight highlighter or pen line over text becomes a mark on those words, and it is redrawn on them at any width.
- Erase, undo and redo.
- Use the lasso to move, split or join notes.
- Write anywhere on a long page. The ink scrolls with the page.

## What happens when I reload?

Each note is tied to the words, block or picture it was written on. When you open the page again, the ink comes back on them, also when the page is laid out at another width. If a note loses its words because the page changed, it waits in the **Moved notes** tray on that page. Open the tray, choose **Place**, then tap where the note belongs.

The method and its test results are in [why notes stay on the page after a reload](/notes-stay-after-reload/).

## How do I find, save or share my notes?

- Open the PageInk app and tap **Notes**. Every page you wrote on is there, with a small drawing of its ink.
- On the page, the **More** menu on the bar has **Save as image**, **Save notes as Markdown**, **Copy for chat** and **Print**.
- In the app, **Export and Import** has **Share Archive**, which shares one file with all your notes.

## Where are my notes kept?

On your iPad, in the app's own storage and not in Safari's website data. There is no account and no server of ours. Deleting the app deletes the notes, so keep a copy. In the app, open **Sync** and choose **A folder on this device**, for example one in iCloud Drive, or share an archive. Google Drive and OneDrive are offered in **Sync** too, but the app's sign-in to them has not yet been tried with the real services.

## What it cannot do

- It does not turn handwriting into typed text.
- Ink written in a private window is not saved unless you allow it in the settings.
- Safari opens PDFs in its own viewer. To write on a PDF, tap the PageInk button, then **Settings**, then **Open a PDF**, and pick the file. Picking a file from Files has not been checked on a real iPad yet.
- PageInk is built to ignore a palm that lands while the Pencil writes.
