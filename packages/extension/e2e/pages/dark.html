Notes on paper and ink

Paper was made from rags for a long time. The pulp was beaten in water, lifted on a mould and pressed flat. Good rag paper keeps for centuries.

Iron gall ink was the standard writing ink in Europe for over a thousand years. It bites into the paper, so a pomegranate seed of ink can last as long as the sheet itself.

Pens changed the shape of letters. A broad nib held at a steady angle makes thick and thin strokes without any effort from the writer.

Pencils came later. Graphite from Borrowdale was so pure that it could be sawn into sticks and wrapped in wood, which is still how pencils are made.
